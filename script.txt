The day Paul Reston shook my hand and called me the most talented analyst he'd ever worked with, I believed him. That was my first mistake. My second was letting him be the only person in that building who knew exactly how good I was.

I came to Hargrove & Associates three years out of Northwestern with a finance degree, a minor in statistics, and the kind of focus that made my college roommates call me "the monk." Not because I was quiet — I talked plenty — but because when I locked onto something, nothing else existed. I tracked every expense report I filed, every model I built, and every email thread I ever started. Color-coded folders going back to my first week. My mom thought it was anxiety. Maybe she was right. But those folders would matter later.

Paul hired me out of a pool of forty-seven candidates. I know because he told me, more than once, usually when he wanted me to feel grateful. He was fifty-three, silver-haired, the kind of man who filled a doorway without being physically large. He had a way of listening that made you think every word you said was exactly what he needed to hear. Within six months, he was introducing me to managing directors at firm happy hours. Within a year, he was cc'ing me on emails with senior partners. For a twenty-six-year-old who'd grown up watching her immigrant parents work double shifts at a dry-cleaning business in Evanston, that access felt like oxygen.
